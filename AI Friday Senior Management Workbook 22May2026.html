Objective

This exercise set is designed for Senior Management participants to demonstrate how AI can strengthen decision-making, improve operational efficiency, enhance stakeholder communication, and accelerate scalable transformation across key business and support functions.

Participants are expected to use AI responsibly to create realistic synthetic inputs, interpret management information, generate leadership-ready outputs, and articulate improvement opportunities in a concise and decision-oriented manner. The exercises are designed to reflect senior management priorities such as governance, business risk, cross-functional dependencies, service performance, escalations, financial and operational impact, and execution discipline.

The process and scenario in this exercise are indicative only; participants may choose any function, delivery, governance, transformation, or operational area they currently oversee and know well, provided the context is relevant for senior management review and decision-making.

✨

Key Areas of Assessment

Comprehensive Application of AI: The use of AI for creating deliverables

Impactful Implementation: Evaluators will look for clear evidence of how AI technologies have positively influenced activity

Deliverable Quality and Completeness: Each deliverable must be thoroughly developed, meeting all specified requirements with high quality and completeness.

Innovative Approach: Out-of-the-box thinking will be valued, especially in the creation of deliverables. For example, submitting a status report in HTML format instead of the traditional Word document or PPT format will be considered innovative.

Exercise 1: Delivery Health Diagnostic & Executive Brief

Domain Context:

Applicable to large IT delivery programs or managed services portfolios with multiple projects/accounts. Includes SLA/KPI tracking, customer satisfaction (CSAT), defect backlog, release timelines, and escalations.

Detailed Guidance:

Create a synthetic delivery portfolio covering multiple projects, tracks, or workstreams within a large program or managed services account. Include a mix of health indicators such as green, amber, and red status across schedule, quality, budget, customer satisfaction, SLA performance, resource utilization, and dependency management. Add realistic leadership concerns such as delayed milestones, repeated defect leakage, high attrition in a critical team, environment instability, or customer dissatisfaction in one account despite acceptable performance in others. For example, one project may be on track functionally but facing margin pressure due to unplanned effort, another may show stable delivery but declining CSAT because of communication gaps, while a third may be missing milestones because of dependency delays or skill shortages. Use AI to analyze the overall portfolio, identify patterns and correlations across projects, surface priority risks, and generate leadership-ready insights on where intervention is needed, what actions should be taken, and how the portfolio can be stabilized or improved.

What Good Looks Like:

Clear prioritization of risks, structured insights, correlation across KPIs, and actionable leadership decisions.

Mandatory Deliverables:

Delivery health report

Dashboard (RAG, trends, risk heatmap…)

Exercise 2: Incident to Problem & Service Improvement

Domain Context:

IT infrastructure or application support teams managing production environments, back-office operations, and related SLAs and service commitments.

Detailed Guidance:

Create a realistic major incident scenario involving application outage, repeated infrastructure or monitoring alerts, incorrect transaction processing, batch failure, performance degradation, or an SLA breach affecting business users or customers. Build the scenario with enough detail to show the business service impacted, timeline of events, symptoms observed, immediate containment actions, and the operational or customer impact. For example, the incident may involve a payment application failing during peak hours, a critical batch not completing on time and delaying downstream processes, repeated database alerts that were initially treated as isolated events but later caused service disruption, or incorrect transaction postings leading to reconciliation issues. Use AI to move beyond incident reporting and perform structured analysis to identify likely root causes, contributing factors, pattern recurrence, and control gaps. Based on the findings, propose preventive actions such as monitoring improvements, automation, process correction, knowledge updates, problem management actions, or architecture and environment fixes so the response clearly shows a shift from reactive restoration to proactive service improvement.

What Good Looks Like:

Strong RCA thinking, shift from reactive to proactive, realistic preventive controls and automation usage.

Mandatory Deliverables:

Incident management document

Incident-to-prevention recommendations

Exercise 3: AI Governance & Data Usage Decision Case

Domain Context:

Applicable to enterprise AI adoption scenarios involving sensitive business, customer, employee, or financial data.

Detailed Guidance:

Create a realistic use case in which business, operational, customer, employee, or financial data is proposed for AI-driven insights, recommendations, or automation. Define enough context to explain what the AI solution is expected to do, what type of data it needs, how the data will be accessed or processed, and what business value is expected. For example, the use case may involve using incident and ticket history to identify recurring service issues, project and financial data to predict margin risks, employee skill and allocation data to support staffing decisions, or customer interaction data to improve response quality and trend analysis. Assess whether the proposed data usage is suitable for production use by examining sensitivity, privacy implications, regulatory or policy constraints, quality of data, access controls, and likelihood of misuse. Based on this assessment, define the required governance measures such as approvals, masking or anonymization, role-based access, audit trails, model usage boundaries, human review points, and other safeguards needed before the solution can be approved for enterprise use.

What Good Looks Like:

Balanced decision-making covering innovation vs compliance, clarity on approvals, and risk mitigation strategy.

Mandatory Deliverables:

Governance decision note

Governance model (data → approval → usage)

Exercise 4: Human + AI Operating Model Redesign

Domain Context:

Applicable across IT delivery, ITOPS, PMO, HR operations, finance operations, or back-office workflows.

Detailed Guidance:

Choose a current process that relies heavily on manual effort, coordination, or repetitive decision-making, and redesign it using AI by clearly splitting activities into human-led, AI-assisted, and fully automated steps. Include enough detail to show how the process works today, where delays, errors, handoff issues, or effort-heavy tasks exist, and what business outcomes need to improve. For example, the process may involve incident triage and ticket assignment in IT operations, weekly project status consolidation across multiple workstreams, onboarding and access provisioning in support functions, PMO action tracking and follow-up, architecture review preparation, or invoice validation in back-office operations. Use AI to identify which tasks should remain with people because they require judgment, stakeholder interaction, approvals, or exception handling; which tasks can be accelerated through AI assistance such as summarization, classification, recommendation, or draft generation; and which tasks can be automated end to end with minimal human intervention. Provide practical guidance on expected benefits such as cycle-time reduction, effort savings, better consistency, improved decision quality, or stronger compliance, along with key assumptions, risks, adoption considerations, and a realistic transition path from current state to future state.

What Good Looks Like:

Clear transformation vision, quantifiable benefits (effort reduction, cycle time), and realistic adoption path.

Mandatory Deliverables:

Process redesign document

Current vs future state view and ROI analysis

Video walkthrough for future state solutions and benefits (3 Mins)

Exercise 5: Efficient Delivery governance

Domain Context:

Applicable to delivery, quality, and PMO teams responsible for maintaining delivery governance, closure discipline, evidence readiness, and management review reporting across projects or accounts.

Detailed Guidance:

Create a delivery governance scenario in which commitments made across project reviews, customer escalations, internal action trackers, and milestone follow-ups are spread across multiple reports, emails, meeting notes, and status decks, resulting in inconsistent status reporting, missed closures, weak traceability, and limited leadership visibility. Build the scenario with enough context to show the type of program or account involved, the nature of the open actions, the teams or owners responsible, the timelines committed, and the business impact of delays or incomplete evidence. For example, the scenario may involve a large program review where actions from architecture, testing, and deployment teams are tracked separately and several critical items remain open without clear ownership; a customer escalation in which closure updates are inconsistent across mail trails and governance decks; or a fixed-price program where delayed risk closures and missing decision logs are affecting milestone readiness and increasing management concern. Design an AI-assisted framework to consolidate actions from multiple sources, validate closure evidence, detect slippages early, highlight overdue items, and generate a reliable governance view for leadership decision-making. Participants should show how AI can support action normalization, ownership tracking, due-date monitoring, reminder generation, closure validation, trend analysis, and review-ready reporting so that governance becomes more proactive, transparent, and execution-focused.

What Good Looks Like:

A structured closure plan, clear ownership and timelines, proactive readiness monitoring, and strong escalation clarity for delivery reviews.

Mandatory Deliverables:

Governance and closure-tracking Framework using AI (Only framework not actual application)

Sample Delivery governance report

Exercise 6: Executive Transformation Pitch

Domain Context:

Applicable to delivery leadership, enterprise or solution architecture, IT operations, transformation initiatives, solutioning, presales, or internal strategic programs.

Detailed Guidance:

Choose a solution, transformation opportunity, or operational improvement from delivery, architecture, or IT operations, and convert it into a CXO-level pitch that clearly explains the business problem, why the opportunity matters now, what strategic value it creates, and how it can be implemented in a practical and scalable way. The pitch should go beyond describing the idea and show how it improves cost, speed, risk control, service quality, customer satisfaction, resilience, or execution discipline. For example, the topic may involve using AI to improve large program governance and executive visibility, modernizing an application estate to reduce technical debt and support faster releases, introducing predictive operations to reduce incidents and SLA breaches, strengthening architecture review with AI-assisted pattern recommendation, or creating a delivery command center that combines financial, operational, and risk signals for leadership decision-making. Participants should clearly articulate the current pain points, proposed solution, expected business outcomes, target stakeholders, required enablers, implementation approach, risks or dependencies, and a realistic value story that would help senior leadership understand why the investment or change should be supported.

What Good Looks Like:

Clear executive storytelling, quantified business value, strategic fit, and strong articulation of delivery, architecture, or IT operations outcomes.

Mandatory Deliverables:

Executive summary

Presentation Deck

Final pitch video (3 Mins)

Exercise 7: Financial Risk & Forecasting

Domain Context:

Applicable to project/program financial governance, budget tracking, and cost optimization scenarios.

Detailed Guidance:

Create a realistic financial scenario for a large program or fixed-price project, including budget, burn rate, cost variance, milestone-linked revenue assumptions, margin pressures, change request dependency, subcontractor or licensing cost exposure, and delivery dependencies that can affect financial outcomes. Build the scenario with enough detail to show the commercial model, baseline plan, actual spend pattern, forecast changes, and the reasons financial performance may be moving off track. For example, the scenario may involve a fixed-price engagement where repeated scope clarifications are consuming effort without approved change requests, a large transformation program where delayed milestones are pushing revenue recognition and increasing bench or subcontractor costs, or a managed service where SLA penalties, productivity loss, or transition overruns are reducing expected margins. Use AI to identify early warning signals, forecast likely financial outcomes under different assumptions, highlight the major risk drivers, and recommend practical mitigation actions such as scope control, milestone replanning, staffing optimization, automation, vendor renegotiation, or governance interventions. Participants should clearly explain the assumptions used, show how the forecast was derived, and provide decision-oriented recommendations that leadership can use to protect revenue, improve margins, and reduce delivery-related financial risk.

What Good Looks Like:

Proactive risk signals, clear forecasting logic, and decision-oriented recommendations.

Mandatory Deliverables:

Financial risk report

Financial dashboard

Exercise 8: Stakeholder Escalation Strategy

Domain Context:

Applicable to delivery programs facing delays, SLA breaches, or customer dissatisfaction.

Detailed Guidance:

Create a realistic escalation scenario for a large delivery program, managed service, or fixed-price engagement in which one or more issues such as milestone slippage, repeated SLA breaches, quality concerns, budget overrun, high defect leakage, environment instability, or delayed dependency closure have triggered customer concern and internal leadership attention. Build the scenario with enough context to show what went wrong, who is impacted, what commitments are at risk, and what recovery actions are required. For example, the scenario may involve a critical release delay affecting a customer launch, repeated production incidents leading to service credits, or a fixed-price project in which effort overruns are reducing margins and increasing stakeholder pressure. Based on this scenario, prepare tailored communication and response plans for customer stakeholders, account or delivery leadership, and internal teams, with clear messaging, accountability, actions, timelines, and escalation handling strategy.

What Good Looks Like:

Clear messaging, stakeholder alignment, and actionable next steps.

Mandatory Deliverables:

Communication notes/mails/reports

Escalation handling strategy

Exercise 9: AI Opportunity Identification

Domain Context:

Applicable to any account, business unit, or operational function seeking AI-led transformation.

Detailed Guidance:

Identify multiple AI use cases across delivery, architecture, IT operations, PMO, support, or business functions, and prioritize them based on business value, implementation feasibility, strategic relevance, and measurable impact. Consider examples such as AI-assisted incident trend analysis, automated project status summarization, intelligent effort or capacity forecasting, architecture pattern recommendation, knowledge search for support teams, or governance action tracking. For each use case, briefly define the business problem, expected benefits, likely users, data or process inputs required, implementation complexity, and key risks or dependencies. Use this analysis to create a simple prioritization view that helps leadership decide which opportunities should be pursued first, which require further validation, and which may be deferred.

What Good Looks Like:

Strong prioritization logic and realistic transformation roadmap.

Opportunity backlog

Prioritization matrix

Exercise 10: Resource & Capacity Optimization

Domain Context:

Applicable to delivery and program managers handling allocation, utilization, and staffing.

Detailed Guidance:

Create a realistic resource and capacity scenario in which a project, program, or support function is facing either underutilization, over-allocation, critical skill shortages, or uneven workload distribution across teams. Include enough context to show the roles involved, current allocation levels, key delivery commitments, and the business impact of the imbalance. For example, the scenario may involve a fixed-price program where a few specialists are overloaded while other team members remain underused, an IT operations team struggling to cover peak incident volumes with limited shift capacity, or a large delivery program where upcoming milestones require skills that are not available in the right locations or timelines. Use AI to analyze patterns, identify resource risks, recommend rebalancing or redeployment options, and improve utilization without affecting delivery commitments or quality. Participants should provide clear assumptions, prioritization logic, and practical recommendations that leadership can use for staffing, planning, or corrective action.

What Good Looks Like:

Balanced workload distribution and improved utilization outcomes.

Mandatory Deliverables:

Resource plan

Capacity visualization

Exercise 11: Open Innovation Challenge – Choose Your Own AI use case or AI Agent Use Case

Domain Context:

Applicable to any participant function, including delivery, architecture, IT operations, PMO, quality, support, governance, or transformation. This exercise is intended to allow participants to bring in a realistic use case from their own day-to-day work and demonstrate how AI or AI agents can improve it. This aligns with the broader shift from using AI only as a tool to using AI assistants, supervised agents, or agent-led workflows for business outcomes.

Detailed Guidance:

Choose any realistic use case from your own day-to-day work that is not fully covered by the earlier exercises, or extend one of the given exercises into a more advanced AI-enabled or agent-led solution. The use case may focus on improving decision-making, reducing manual effort, accelerating coordination, increasing governance visibility, improving service quality, strengthening stakeholder communication, or enabling better planning and execution.

Participants may choose either of the following approaches:

Option 1

AI-assisted task execution

Show how a human can use AI tools to complete the work faster, better, or more consistently.

Option 2

AI agent or multi-agent workflow design

Show how one or more agents can perform part of the work with human supervision, approvals, guardrails, and exception handling.

The use case should address a real day-to-day business problem using synthetic inputs and clearly show the current challenge, the role of AI or agents, the areas requiring human oversight, the needed controls, and the expected business value.

Examples include governance packs, project or program status summaries, action tracking, incident or ticket analysis, architecture review support, effort forecasting, stakeholder communication, PMO follow-up, release readiness, compliance evidence consolidation, or onboarding support.

Participants may also show how AI works as a supervised assistant or agent workflow. For example, one agent may collect inputs, another may summarize them, and a third may prepare the final output for human review.
